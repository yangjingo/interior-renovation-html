# Three-floor circulation scenarios (anonymized)

This public note records how the proposed layout is meant to work in daily life. It omits the client's plans, room dimensions, drawing crops, title blocks, renders and detailed review HTML. The private design guide carries the full room-by-room discussion. Every route below is a **design proposal** until checked against the current architectural, structural and MEP drawings and site measurements.

For each scene, check the route with the furniture **in use**: doors open, chairs pulled out, wardrobe leaves open, windows operated and equipment accessible. A render's apparent empty space is not a measured clearance.

## First floor — arrival, hospitality, meals and ground-floor rest

The proposed layout separates a public arrival and dining sequence from the bedroom and bathroom route. A closable kitchen keeps cooking connected to the dining room while allowing fumes and noise to be contained. The stair remains visible and free of temporary storage.

| Daily scene | Intended route and placement | Check before construction or purchase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arriving with shoes, umbrellas and groceries | Deal with wet items and shoes beside the entrance. Split groceries toward the kitchen and people toward seating, bedrooms or the stair without stopping in the central passage. | Check entrance-door swing, cabinet doors, threshold, drainage and two-person passing with the actual storage units. |
| Cooking and serving | Organize the kitchen around food storage, washing, preparation, cooking and a short serving route to the table. A closable transparent boundary can maintain visual contact. | Keep the door panel's parked position clear of pulled-out dining chairs. Verify the existing opening, ventilation and services; do not assume an enlarged opening is approved. |
| Hosting a group | Let visitors reach seating, dining and the shared bathroom without crossing a bedroom. Keep formal seating to the sides of the arrival axis. | Test the dining table at maximum intended occupancy and the bathroom queue with chairs pulled back. |
| Everyday living-room use | Keep sofa, coffee table and screen in a defined seating area; pass around it toward dining and the entrance. | Check screen reflection, window operation, curtains, side-table placement and cleaning access. |
| Ground-floor bedroom use | Keep a direct bedroom-to-bathroom route for someone who should avoid stairs; allow access around the bed and to the wardrobe. | Measure the actual bed, door, wardrobe and threshold arrangement. Assess accessibility against the resident's needs rather than inferring it from images. |
| Night bathroom trip | Lead from bed to the appropriate bathroom along the side passage with low-glare guidance light. | Check opened doors, slip resistance, wet-area layout and ventilation. A small bathroom should not receive an unverified fixed glass enclosure. |
| Cleaning and waste removal | Move cleaning items and kitchen waste back toward the known entrance while preserving the central passage. | Keep appliance doors, sink maintenance and electrical access available; do not invent a rear service exit or drain. |
| Carrying luggage between floors | Approach the stair from the arrival area without crossing dining chairs or movable furniture. | Verify the real stair turns, handrails, landings and lighting before selecting large furniture. |

## Second floor — family lounge, bedrooms and a proposed kitchen/bar

The existing drawings show a different use for the northwest room and adjacent balcony than the newer open-kitchen and window-bar concept. This is the largest unresolved circulation and service conflict on the floor. The family lounge should still connect the stair, bedrooms, bathroom group and south balcony without sending everyone through the bar seating area.

| Daily scene | Intended route and placement | Check before construction or purchase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Morning wash | Let bedroom occupants reach the shared wash area and bathrooms without door collisions or a queue blocking the lounge. | Verify the actual bathroom doors, dry/wet zones and simultaneous use. |
| Light breakfast | Reach the proposed kitchen from the stair or bedrooms, then sit by the window. Stools tuck under the bar when nobody is seated. | Verify the room-use change, existing window/enclosure and all required services before fixing the bar. |
| Two people preparing food | One person uses storage and preparation surfaces while another takes drinks or food toward the bar. | Check island, refrigerator and cabinet doors with stools pulled out; the balcony drain is not proof of a kitchen waste connection. |
| Family lounge and children playing | Place the main seating and play area beside the through-route from stair to the south balcony. | Keep toys, rugs, plants and lamps out of the balcony-door and bathroom approaches. |
| Bedroom reading or work | Reach each bed, desk, window and wardrobe without one item blocking another. | Measure furniture and door leaves room by room. Do not move a window simply to improve a render. |
| Overnight guest bathroom trip | Lead the guest to the shared bathroom without crossing the primary bedroom. | Check low light, floor slip risk and bathroom-door operation. |
| Laundry and drying | Start with a confirmed laundry location and trace clothes, water supply, drain, ventilation and drying to their actual destinations. | The existing laundry-balcony role conflicts with the proposed bar. Resolve that conflict in coordinated drawings before assigning a final laundry route. |
| Cleaning and maintenance | Keep access to the kitchen appliances, bathroom drains, windows, electrical equipment and balcony threshold. | Do not hide service points behind fixed joinery. |
| Night descent or emergency exit | Reach the stair from every bedroom with the lounge passage clear. | Verify landing, handrail, lighting and applicable egress requirements. |

## Third floor — quiet work, shared bathroom, private closet and roofless garden

The proposed use retains one bathroom for the floor and converts a former bathroom into a dry closet serving the exterior-window primary bedroom. The garden remains outside. Its legal and physically workable entrance has **not** been confirmed; no route below assumes a new door from the lounge or study.

| Daily scene | Intended route and placement | Check before construction or purchase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arriving upstairs | Move from the stair into the lounge and then to the study, bedrooms, shared bathroom or south balcony. | Keep the landing legible and unobstructed; verify guard and handrail details. |
| Primary bedroom dressing | Move from the bed around usable bedside space into the proposed private closet, then return through the bedroom to the common route. | Check wardrobe doors, bed size, ventilation, former plumbing closure and the proposed bedroom-side opening with the responsible designers. |
| Shared bathroom at peak times | Reach the retained bathroom from common space; keep washing, toilet and shower use distinct. | Resolve dry/wet boundaries, drainage, waterproofing, exhaust and door swing in a revised drawing. Do not reuse a superseded two-bathroom merge concept. |
| Dual-screen work or a video call | Enter the study, sit or stand at the desk, then reach the bathroom or lounge without stepping over cables. | Check chair pull-out, door swing, daylight, cooling, outlets and network points. |
| Gaming followed by quiet reading | Concentrate equipment and noise in the study; keep the lounge as a separate short-stay area. | Verify ventilation, power load, lighting and sound transfer. |
| Guest staying overnight | Give the guest bedroom a route to the shared bathroom that does not enter the primary closet. | Check the guest room's bed, luggage, wardrobe and door use together. |
| Sitting in the garden | **After** the architect confirms the actual entrance, move from that entrance to a central open area and edge seating. | Verify the entrance, threshold, guard, exterior envelope, waterproofing and outdoor power. |
| Rain and planting maintenance | Reach drains and overflow points by moving light pots and furniture aside. | Have structure and drainage specialists assess wet planting loads, falls and maintenance; avoid heavy fixed planters pending review. |
| Service-balcony cleaning | Use its existing confirmed access for equipment and cleaning without conflating it with the roofless garden. | Keep floor drains, windows, guards and inspection points accessible. |
| Night descent or emergency exit | Reach the stair directly from the rooms and lounge; the private closet is a destination, not a shortcut. | Verify guidance light, clear doors, stair dimensions and applicable egress requirements. |

## Release boundary

The public repository carries reusable circulation rules and this anonymized scenario summary. The private, image-rich HTML guide and construction-drawing review are separate deliverables. Before implementation, the design team should mark each door, window, route, service point and clearance as confirmed, revised or pending site/professional verification.
